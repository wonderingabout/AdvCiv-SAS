== K-Mod to AdvCiv Changes ==

Provided by AdvCiv-SAS maintainer, and non-exhaustive list of some info i'd want to display there as well, from AdvCiv's manual.pdf or similar path for manual.txt or some other AdvCiv-related sources.

Notable adjustments to K-Mod AI behavior (non-exhaustive as well):
- AI razes fewer cities and less randomly; 116
- uses Slavery and Drafting less aggressively, though still frequently; 121b, 017
- uses destructive espionage missions only against civs it dislikes; 120
- will rarely found a religion before a second city (reinforcing K-Mod changes that make the AI less interested in the early religions). 171

Reverted by AdvCiv (i (AdvCiv-SAS maintainer/developer) assume from K-Mod (non-exhaustive as well)):
- See Immortal Culture about reverted changes to culture and revolts.
- Production overflow is never invested into an additional unit as this had lead to balance problems with AI civs training multiple Archers per turn. Instead, excess overflow is converted into gold as in the Unofficial Patch, though with some tweaks to make this conversion more difficult to abuse. 064b
- Gifting Great People to the AI does not provide a relations bonus. 141
- No impact of global research on inflation; instead, adjusted tech costs based on handicap and the years-per-turn progression for a more historically accurate tech pace, and Immortal and Deity games now start on turn 10. 251, 910
-  Some minor balance changes undone that weren't pulling their weight in terms of added complexity (change 200) (non-exhaustive (AdvCiv-SAS maintainer/develoepr note)): All corporations have the same maintenance multiplier (i.e. mostly as in BtS)

Important miscellaneous changes in AdvCiv (non-exhaustive as well)
- Workers and settlers only have a 50% chance of being captured and 0% if attacked on the same turn as declaring war. 010
- When a tile with unfinished worker builds is left alone for more than 7 turns, the invested worker turns begin to decay. This is mainly to disincentivize pre-chopping. 011
- Forest and Jungle provide only 25% defense, and none if the tile is owned by the attacker. 012
- Unowned Forests and Jungles can't be chopped down. 119
- The AI evacuates land units that can't defend well from cities that are about to be attacked and untenable. 139
- When the AI has more happiness or health than it needs, it may refuse to accept resources in trade or may trade away its only copy of a resource. 036
- Undefended cities stop "fearing for their safety" once Nationalism is discovered. 500c
- Slavery base yield reduced to 24 production (from 30). 912d
- The Spy unit can investigate rival cities, but (unlike in Warlords) with a mission cost. 103
- Research progress toward a tech makes that tech cheaper to steal. 120i
- When a citizen starves, the Granary is emptied to delay further starvation. 160
- The Apostolic Palace can propose war against (non-full) voting members, but only full members are compelled to declare war. kekm.25
- Can no longer bypass the Theocracy restriction by gifting missionaries. 123a/ kekm.4
- Damage from nuclear weapons to units made less predictable; SDI interception chance reduced from 75% to 60% and cost increased from 1000 to 1500 production. 650
- Wonders that have been manually removed from the production queue generate no "fail gold." Also no gold from national units. 123f
- Trade routes can only come from revealed cities, and the paths to those cities need to be revealed as well. Cities are temporarily unavailable for trade when in disorder. Trade along rivers doesn't require any tech (as in Warlords; BtS demands Sailing). When borders of a war enemy block trade, a naval blockade can override this. Connecting a resource on an island workable by a mainland city requires only a road, not a fort. 124
- When mountain peaks block off part of a landmass, the parts are treated as separate continents. This is relevant for the placement of Barbarians, colony maintenance and wonders that affect only one continent - though the main point is to make things easier for the AI. 030
- Land threshold for Domination victory reduced, especially in large games. 254
- On Emperor handicap and above, some of the AI discounts have been reduced, but human civs face increased production costs, research costs, city growth thresholds and Great Person thresholds. The AI also starts with fewer freebies, in particular, without Agriculture on Immortal and Deity and without a second free Settler on Deity. Progressive AI bonuses are now based on the game turn instead of the AI era. 251/ 250e
- Marathon speed is only 2.5 times slower than Normal speed, not 3 times. 252
- The default player count is 8 for the Standard map size. 137
- Map generation: Clusters of Gold, Gems made less common. Silver may occur on Grassland Forest. No Flood Plains on river corners. Jungle may occur on Plains. 129
- New algorithm for assigning starting locations. Should partition the available land more evenly than in BtS. Not enabled for all map scripts (list). 027, 108
- Switched the no-war-probability of Roosevelt and Darius, meaning that Darius can start wars when pleased, and Roosevelt - normally - cannot. 005a
- Suryavarman's favorite religion is Hinduism and his favorite civic Caste System. 005a

== Mod Version / Source ==

This page shows the source identity of the currently running mod and, when a game is loaded, the source-revision history persisted by that save. The practical AdvCiv-SAS version is the Git commit-count version. A full Git checkout can also verify branch and tracked dirty state; exported GitHub/git-archive copies retain source commit/version metadata but use unavailable dirty state because post-extraction edits cannot be verified without a Git worktree.

Saved history records creation and later transitions when practical version or full commit SHA changes. Dirty state is preserved as context but does not by itself create a transition.

Here, dirty refers specifically to tracked Git files in a full checkout. Clean (0) means no tracked files differ from the recorded commit; dirty (1) means at least one tracked file differs, so the displayed commit identifies the checkout HEAD/baseline but does not necessarily describe every current file; unavailable (-1) means the state cannot be verified (normally because the mod came from an extracted archive without a Git worktree), not that it is clean or dirty. Untracked files are intentionally ignored. The dirty-file summary preserves Git's two status columns: the first is the index/staged state and the second is the working-tree/unstaged state. The summary may be shortened for readability while Dirty tracked files keeps the full tracked-file count.

Hall of Fame Legend (CONCEPT_SAS_HALL_OF_FAME_LEGEND)
######################################################################
== Hall of Fame ==

Hall of Fame lists games read from .CivBeyondSwordReplay files, normally in Documents/My Games/Beyond the Sword/Replays. Use the drop-down filters to narrow the list; click a table column heading to sort it.

== Columns ==

- V: View replay. Click the row's V button to open that game's replay; this control is omitted when the game has just ended.
- Leader portrait and civilization icon: identify the player; click for the corresponding Sevopedia page where available.
- Col: player color marker.
- Leader: recorded player/leader and civilization names.
- T1 / T2: first and second trait icons from the leader's current XML definition.
- Normalized: normalized score used for comparing games.
- Finish Date: in-game finishing date (BC/AD), not the real-world date when the game was played.
- Score: final game score before normalization.
- Victory: win/loss indication and victory type when identified; W means win and L means loss.
- Handicap: difficulty level.
- Size: world size.
- Start: starting era.
- Speed: game speed, using its current XML description.

== Older replay files and mod changes ==

AdvCiv-SAS 6671 fixes the rejection of SAS's -50000 start year and distinguishes newly written SAS replay files from AdvCiv's. Hall of Fame can therefore list games from older SAS replay files as well as newly recorded ones; AdvCiv and other-mod files are excluded with the default settings.

Older files are interpreted using today's XML definitions, not a snapshot of the mod when the game was played. They store numeric positions for settings such as game speed, world size, era, handicap and leader. Adding, deleting or reordering entries can change what those positions mean. For example, an old game-speed position 2 can identify a different speed after an earlier entry is inserted or deleted. A position still within the current range can display the wrong label; an invalid position can cause the file to be rejected.

Leader portraits and trait icons use current leader definitions; civilization icons are matched from recorded civilization names against current XML. Changed traits, art or civilization names can therefore affect their appearance or availability. Not every XML change affects replays: they do not store the complete unit or building database.

Keep these display limits in mind when comparing games recorded under different versions of the mod.

For the investigation and technical details, see KI#166.2 (replay identity/filtering) and KI#166.3 (calendar bounds) in AdvCiv-SAS's README_Known_Issues.md.

World Advisor Territory Legend (CONCEPT_SAS_WORLD_ADVISOR_TERRITORY_LEGEND)

== World Advisor Territory Legend ==

The Territory tab counts plots and map assets owned by the selected player.

- BFC: owned plots inside at least one selected-player city BFC.
- Sub: suburbs; owned plots outside all selected-player city BFCs.
- Tot: BFC plus Sub.

In Plots, Water, Peak, Hill and Flat partition all owned plots, so Total is their real plot count. River, Lake and Coastal overlap those rows; for example, a riverside Grassland Hill is both Hill and River. Terrain and Features are split because every plot has one terrain, while only some have a feature.

Hidden or unexplored plots and bonuses stay hidden unless debug mode is active. Hover row icons for tooltips, or click them to open Sevopedia entries when available.

Changes Tab Legend (CONCEPT_SAS_WORLD_ADVISOR_CHANGES_LEGEND)
######################################################################
== Changes Tab ==

The Changes tab combines recorded Advanced Start actions and WorldBuilder edits in one table; the Source column distinguishes them. The ordered history is stored in the save even when SASGameRecord logging is disabled. Loading a save made before an edit restores the earlier history; it does not recover later actions from a log.

== Reading the Columns ==

- # follows saved action order. The default display is newest first; column headers can change the sort order. Repeated WorldBuilder experience, level, population, culture and gold setter rows may appear as one compact row when they are contiguous, target the same object/property and form an exact old-to-new chain. The row uses the last raw #, Action shows xN, and Before / After span the first and final applied values. Intervening actions always break a group. Set SAS_WORLD_CHANGES_SHOW_INTERMEDIATE_ON to 1 to show every permitted raw edit separately; its default 0 keeps repeated spinner adjustments compact. This is presentation-only; the save and SGR retain every applied edit. Manual editor steps are usually few and can help explain unexpected results; the compact view reduces visual clutter without discarding that debugging evidence.
- Turn / Year identify when the edit happened in the game, not the computer's clock.
- S (Source) uses A for Advanced Start and W for WorldBuilder to leave more room for actions and targets. LOG exports the full source names. P identifies the editor or acting player by numeric player ID (also used by the Actor field in LOG); it need not be the owner of the affected unit, city or plot. The adjacent civilization icon and leader button describe that player slot's current identity, not a saved historical identity. Civilization, leader and player ID sort independently, including when several player slots share the same leader. City targets have a city icon when no item-specific icon applies.
- Action / Target identify the recorded operation and affected item. Action icons reuse WorldBuilder tool art, with category icons when no exact match exists; they provide inspection help, not an editor command. Target icons use current XML artwork/help and open the matching Sevopedia page when one exists. Missing historical types and non-XML targets retain text/ID help. X / Y give known coordinates in independently sortable numeric columns, beside the jump button at the far right; its hover repeats the coordinates and camera hint.
- Before / After show available values before and after the edit, in separate sortable numeric columns. Some values are numeric XML or enum IDs; -1 can be a valid absence value, such as no improvement. A dash means no displayed value. Withheld means the name changed, or script text capture was disabled when that edit was recorded. Enabled script captures show the applied data in escaped notation, not a script filename; the viewer never executes it.
- Pts B / Pts A and the gold-symbol B / A columns show Advanced Start points and gold before (B) and after (A) the action. Gold/culture value edits and extra-yield targets also use their corresponding glyphs; generic Before / After values have no fixed unit. Cost is its quoted point cost, including the quote associated with a refund. Each column sorts independently by numeric value, like the numeric Battles columns; click a header to sort. Begin ends setup (Pts A = -1) and converts remaining points into gold. A dash means not applicable/unavailable, not zero; these budget fields do not represent a WorldBuilder purchase price.

== Visibility and Controls ==

- Ordinary play filters hidden details; debug mode exposes the complete record. Hidden is a redacted value, not proof that no action occurred.
- The plot icon closes the advisor and centers the camera on an available recorded location. LOG exports the permitted raw applied-event rows to PythonDbg.log, including individual WorldBuilder scalar edits that the table may compact. SAS_WORLD_CHANGES_SHOW_CANCELLED_ON=0 (default) omits matching Advanced Start unit/population/building buy-refund pairs only when recorded identities, quotes and point/gold balances match; nested pairs can cancel in reverse order. It also omits compact WorldBuilder scalar runs whose first and final values match. Set it to 1 to keep those cancelled rows visible. Hidden/omitted entries and other action families are barriers. This summarizes recorded count/value and budget effects, not all gameplay side effects, such as explored plots or city food/counters. SHOW_INTERMEDIATE takes precedence and displays every permitted raw event. Saved history, SGR and LOG remain unchanged.
- Reveal-all/unreveal-all normally appears as one row whose After value counts affected plots. PARTIAL marks an interrupted bulk operation; it does not identify every changed coordinate.

Scalar compaction uses only persisted contiguous-event evidence; these saved setter rows are already applied mutations, and the history does not persist a distinct editor OK/close boundary. Closing and reopening the same editor field without any intervening recorded action can therefore still display as one compact run. Map brushes, bulk plot changes, ownership/diplomacy and destructive edits are not scalar-compacted. The separate cancellation option excludes Advanced Start city deletion, culture, technology, visibility and map replacements because the recorded fields do not establish an inverse.

This records supported action paths rather than every possible editor effect. Sign/landmark captions are outside this history. Names retain only a change marker. Script data is retained by default; SAS_WORLD_CHANGES_SCRIPT_TEXT_ON=0 records only a withheld marker for subsequent edits. Earlier withheld content cannot be recovered by enabling the setting.

== Domestic Advisor Legend ==

The Overview tabs show cities for the selected player. In debug mode, the player dropdown can inspect another alive player's cities.

== Overview 1 ==

- Rel: religions present.
- T: turns left on current production.
- Defense icon %: city defense.
- Strength icon: defenders.
- Occupation icon %: revolt chance.

== Overview 2 ==

- d: net surplus or deficit.
- Food columns: produced food, consumed food, food d, stored food / storage threshold.
- Production columns: produced hammers, stored hammers / production threshold.
- Happiness columns: happy, unhappy, happiness d.
- Health columns: healthy, unhealthy, health d.
- Yield modifier icons %: food, production and commerce yield modifiers.
- Commerce modifier icons %: gold, research, culture and espionage modifiers.
- Trade icon R%: trade route modifier.
- Trade icon FR%: foreign trade route modifier.

== Overview 3 ==

- Culture columns: total, threshold, rate, turns.
- Great Person columns: total, threshold, base rate, final rate, modifier %, turns.
- #: total Great People born in the city.
- Bad-gold %: maintenance modifier.
- WW%: war weariness modifier.
- HA%: hurry anger modifier.
- Unhealthy icon %: unhealthy population modifier.
- Mil%: military production modifier.
- Space%: spaceship production modifier.
- Air%: air defense modifier.
- Nuke%: nuclear defense modifier.
- Espionage icon D%: espionage defense modifier.

== Overview 4 ==

- Founded: founding year.
- POP#: real population.
- Corp: corporations present.
- XP: free unit experience.
- SXP: specialist free experience.
- Specialist portraits: assigned specialists plus free specialists.
- Angry icon: angry citizens.
- X / Y: city coordinates, each independently sortable.

== Sorting and Colors ==

- Signed values sort numerically.
- Positive helpful values are green, negative helpful values are red, and penalty modifiers invert that coloring.
- Zero balance columns can show - for easier scanning, while zero modifier columns are blank.

== Foreign Advisor Espionage Legend ==

The left list shows known rival civilizations and your espionage allocation against each team.

- + / -: increases or decreases this rival's espionage spending weight. Right-clicking uses the larger min/max adjustment.
- Name (%): leader name and current espionage mission cost modifier against that rival. Lower percentages mean missions are cheaper; higher percentages mean missions are more expensive. Extra * or + markers after the percentage come from active counterespionage effects.
- Weight: relative share of your espionage spending assigned to that rival. Example: if two rivals have weights 3 and 1, the first gets about three times as many EPs per turn before rounding.
- EPs: total espionage points accumulated against that rival.
- +N per Turn: current espionage points assigned to that rival each turn.

In large-player games, the target list uses compact rows so the controls stay visible and clickable. Compact rows omit the repeated espionage commerce icon, the repeated "Weight:" label, and the repeated "Cost" text because repeating them on every row makes the list harder to read and uses space needed by the + / - controls.

The right side shows city selection, passive espionage thresholds, available missions, and mission costs for the selected rival.

######################################################################
Military Advisor Battles Legend (CONCEPT_SAS_MILITARY_ADVISOR_BATTLES_LEGEND)
######################################################################
== Military Advisor Battles Legend ==

Battle rows are shown from the inspected player's perspective: "Our" columns always describe the inspected player's unit, "Their" columns describe the opponent. The dropdown selects whose perspective is shown.

== Columns ==

- Turn / Year: when the battle happened.
- Result (emoji): three distinct emojis, one for each outcome: won, lost, or retreat (combat ended without a death).
- Est%: simple strength-share estimate from starting effective strengths: our strength divided by both units' combined strength. It is not exact Civ4 combat odds because first strikes, withdrawal, damage rounds, etc. are not modeled here.
- Role (emoji): emoji shown when the inspected player initiated the attack; blank when defending or when role is unknown.
- Strength icon b: base XML strength of the unit (no modifiers).
- Strength icon s: starting effective strength at combat begin (with all modifiers applied).
- Strength icon e: ending effective strength after combat damage was applied.
- Strength icon m: maximum effective strength (full health, with modifiers).
- XP: unit experience before combat; yellow XP marks a Great-General-led unit.
- Our Unit / Their Unit: the units that fought; click to open Sevopedia entry. Unit name shown in yellow when that unit was led by a Great General.
- Civ / Leader icons (Their side only): opponent civ and leader; click to open Sevopedia entry.
- PID: opponent player ID.
- Cap#: colored capture count from inspected-player perspective. Green = we captured this many of their units; red = they captured this many of ours; blank = no capture.
- Cap (icon): captured unit type when applicable; click to open Sevopedia entry.
- Hill/Peak / Terrain / Feature (optional, gated by SAS define): plot context where the combat occurred; click to open Sevopedia entry.
- X / Y (optional): independently sortable numeric coordinates, enabled by SAS_MILIT_UI_BATTLE_XY_ON. Enabled by default; set the define to 0 to preserve space at 1080p, especially with enlarged text. Coordinates remain available on the Plot icon hover.
- Plot (icon): hover for coordinates and the camera hint; click to close the advisor and center the camera on the battle plot. Three distinct icons cover open-field battle, defended city (still standing for original owner), and city fell during this battle.

== Strength Color Coding ==

- Effective strength columns are color-coded relative to the unit's max effective strength: white at full, green above 66%, yellow above 33%, red at 33% or below.

== Sorting and Logging ==

- Click any column header to sort numerically/alphabetically.
- The LOG button (when enabled via SAS define) writes the visible battle history to PythonDbg.log for external inspection.

== Blank Values ==

- Blank cells can mean the value is zero or not applicable (for example, the lost unit died, no worker was captured), or that the row was not recorded fully for some reason (early version of this feature, bug to fix, etc.).

######################################################################
Military Advisor Summary Legend (CONCEPT_SAS_MILITARY_ADVISOR_SUMMARY_LEGEND)
######################################################################
== Military Advisor Summary Legend ==

The Summary tab consolidates support cost, army composition / quality, and deployment data at a glance. Most numbers were already in Civ4 but scattered across the Finance hover, Composition tab, and city screens; this tab brings the headline data into one panel and exposes the actual DLL formulas so "why is my cost X?" reads directly instead of by guesswork.

== Support Column - Unit Cost Formula ==

- Total Units / Free Used/Cap / Paid: total owned units, free units used out of the free-unit cap (handicap + base + population %), and paid units (count beyond the cap).
- P x R x M: regular unit cost = Paid x getGoldPerUnit() x getUnitCostMultiplier() / 10000. K-Mod stores the rates as percent-like integers so the visible product can floor to 0 even with positive paid units.
- Mil. Cost Units / No Mil. Cost Units: subset of Total Units that pays / bypasses the military surcharge (bMilitarySupport flag in CIV4UnitInfos.xml). Some units, civilian or military, have no military cost (e.g. as of now Worker, Robotic Infantry - see their Sevopedia entry).
- Mil. Cost P x R: military surcharge = Paid Military x getGoldPerMilitaryUnit() / 100. Stacks on top of regular unit cost.
- Extra Cost: getExtraUnitCost() / 100, per-unit overrides.
- U Cost x I: pre-inflation unit cost x inflation factor = displayed Unit Cost.
- Outside Units: units outside own territory. Free / Paid threshold from INITIAL_FREE_OUTSIDE_UNITS define plus game-speed adjustments.
- Outside P x R: Paid Outside x INITIAL_OUTSIDE_UNIT_GOLD_PERCENT / 100. AI handicap can apply an extra multiplier shown as raw then adjusted.
- U Supply x I: pre-inflation unit supply x inflation factor = displayed Unit Supply.
- Total Cost + Supply: Unit Cost + Unit Supply, the bottom-line gold/turn.

== Support Column - City Stats ==

- Military Buildings: Barracks / Stables / Drydocks show built / total cities (Drydocks denominator is coastal cities), or "-" until you have the prerequisite tech. Heroic Epic / Military Academy show "-" until teched, then the owning city name when built, else "0 / 1" (national wonders cap at one). The XP suffix on Barracks (+3), Stables (+X Mounted), Drydocks (+X Naval) is read directly from the building XML.
- Unit Production: Avg Modifier averages getMilitaryProductionModifier() across cities; Best City highlights the strongest single bonus. This aggregate includes XML iMilitaryProductionModifier sources above 0 such as civics, but individual sources are not listed because the Summary tab is already full. Avg New XP / Best New XP read getFreeExperience() per city (Barracks XP, civics, religion, traits all stack into it).

== Army Column ==

- Military Units / Civilian Units: split by canFight() (combat-capable). Civilian units (Workers, Settlers, Spies, Missionaries, Great People) still count toward regular Unit Cost.
- Land / Sea / Air: military units split by getDomainType().
- Strongest: unit type with the highest base XML strength (or air combat for air units). Costliest: highest base hammer cost.
- Total Hammers / Avg Hammers: sum and mean of getProductionCost() across military (canFight) units only; civilians are excluded so they don't dilute army value. Narrower than Power Rank below (which is not hammer-based). Hammer cost works as an alternative power proxy alongside base strength, since Civ4 layers combat modifiers (collateral, city attack, withdraw, first strikes) that base strength alone does not reflect. For example, Trebuchet has lower base strength than concurrent melee units of its era but stronger city-attack value, which the hammer cost prices in.
- Power Rank (Known): your getPower() rank among met civs. getPower() is the engine power rating: summed XML power values of units, power-bearing buildings, techs, and population, not just military hammer cost; broader than Total Hammers (military unit production cost only).
- Avg XP / Avg Level: averaged across military units only; civilians don't dilute.
- Great Generals: standalone GG units (UnitInfo.getLeaderPromotion() set) waiting to attach. GG-Led Units: combat units carrying PROMOTION_LEADER after a prior attach.
- Top Promotions: 5 most common promotions across your military, with count and %-of-military. Icon next to each row.
- Covert Units: counts of units with getInvisibleType() (submarines, etc.), isSpy() (spies), isHiddenNationality() (privateers). Named "covert" rather than "special" because Civ4 already uses "special" for a distinct concept (special unit classes, special buildings). Always shown so the section's existence is discoverable; a zero state is informative.
- Upgradeable: count of units with at least one available upgrade you can build. Min/Avg/Max/Total Cost: cheapest upgrade prices summed across upgradeable units.
- Allied Hammers: total hammer cost across allied civs' military (teammates + mutual vassalage). Allies are "semi-ours" strategically; counts your shared force.

== Deployment Column ==

- In Own Cities: count + Best Defended (city with highest getBuildingDefense()) and Avg Defense (mean across own cities).
- In Own Territory: in own borders but not in a city.
- In Allied / Neutral Civ / Enemy Territory: foreign-civ plot owners split by diplomatic relation. Allied = same team or mutual vassalage. Enemy = at war. Neutral = any other met civ (open-borders partners and defensive-pact partners land here).
- Roaming (Unowned Land) / At Sea (Open Water): unowned plots, land vs water.
- Defenses: counts of Walls / Castles built / total cities, with building icons.
- Wounded: count of military units with any damage, + % of military.
- Max / Avg / Min Health: hit-points-based health %; colored 100% = white, above 66% = green, above 33% = yellow, else red. Same thresholds as the Battles tab strength coloring.
- Full / High / Medium / Low Health: distribution by band (count + % of military). Band rows are intentionally not colored - the values are counts, not health percentages.

== Deployment Column - Battle Record ==

- Battles / Won / Retreated / Lost: aggregated from the persisted SASBattleHistory log (same data feeding the Battles tab). Outcome icons match the Battles tab (white star = won, left arrow = retreated, broken heart = lost).
- Good-Luck Wins: wins where our pre-battle odds were below 50% - we were the underdog and won anyway.
- Bad-Luck Losses: losses where our pre-battle odds were above 50% - we were favored and lost.
- Best: the win with the lowest pre-battle odds (most underdog). Worst: the loss with the highest pre-battle odds (most expected to win).
- Both Best/Worst rows show the two unit icons (ours + theirs), starting effective strengths, year, and final odds %, or "-" until a qualifying battle has been recorded.

== Color Coding ==

- Money lines (Unit Cost / Unit Supply / Total Cost + Supply): green = 0 cost, red = positive cost. Earlier "yellow if income still net-positive" coloring was dropped because this panel explains where the burden comes from, not whether it's affordable in isolation.
- Paid count rows: default white (just a count, "paid" doesn't mean "unaffordable").
- Health %: 100 = white, above 66 = green, above 33 = yellow, else red.
- Battle outcomes: green = won, yellow = retreated, red = lost.

== Indent / Icon Conventions ==

- Sub-rows step right under their parent ("Free / Paid" under "Total Units"). The right-aligned value steps inward too, so a parent's number sits visually above where its children sum to.
- Icons sit at the row's right edge; the value text wraps to their left. Two-icon rows (Best / Worst battles) pair our unit + their unit.

== Perspective ==

- The player dropdown selects whose army is summarized. Outside debug mode this is constrained to you and your vassals (when the SAS define allows). All values are computed from the engine's state for that civ, with no fog-of-war filtering.

== AI Personality Panel Legend ==

- 1st column: "label", a shortened or full descriptive text, like/such as "T.W Likely" (a shortened explanation of what iMaxWarRand means ingame), as said earlier. This column contains the raw value too, for exhaustiveness, for example "T.W Likely (50)" for moctezuma, 50 is the raw value, or "T.W Likely (400)" for Gandhi, 400 is the raw value, for raw ai atributes. For aggregated ai aattributes, like contact probs, positive and negative memory resentments and affections, etc, the raw value is also displayed, but since there are many components (raw values) used to calculate the aggregated ai attribute, we instead display each of them "/" separated, for example for gandhi in the negative memory resentment category, the "D.W onFr (-200/120)" line shows that Gandhi has a raw ai attribute memory attitude percent of -200 (i.e. Gandhi resents quite strongly you declaring war on his friends) and a memory decay of 120. And for Moctezuma, for the same "D.W onFr" negative memory resentment, Moctezuma does not resent as much (memory attitude percent of -100, and memory decay same though of 120 (currently btw decays are same among all leaders (in civ4/advciv and thus advciv-sas too), not ideal but maybe functional))

- 2nd column: the normalized score is displayed. As we explained before at the ranking calculation part, these scores are normalized, which means score is minimum 0 and maximum 100. Sometimes all leaders will have 50 (if same value among all leaders, happens sometimes in civ4 not ideal), and else leaders are ranked gradually from 0 (worse performer for better or worse) to 100 (best performer. Moctezuma performs good at war attributes, and gandhi good at peace attributes, for example). Note that this code is entirely dynamic, you can add more leaders and they'll get the new values and participate and possibly completely upset the best/worse performers ranking depending on their values (if they are different from current leaders ones, and how much), and you could also additionally tweak the existing leaders xml values. (If you think for example the roman leaders underperform in war, you may tweak their xml. As for aggregated ai attributes, they also display normalized scores, but since they are already normalized at their creation by definition, before leaders_data.py is (has finished being) generated), they are still displayed as normalized scores (min 0, max 100, unless all leaders have an equal score/value then all leaders get 50), but since they are already normalized, they are not re-normalized during caching and Sevopedia Leader computation. In short, they can be intepreted similarly as raw ai attributes.

- (Inverted): when a line is marked (Inverted), lower raw values are better/more intense and higher raw values are weaker/less likely. Example: T.W Likely (getMaxWarRand) at 50 means much more war-prone than 400.

- 3rd column: scale, this is an indicative display, raw ai attributes and aggregated ai attributes are graded graphically 10 by 10 with a symbol, but raw ai attributes use a "+" symbol, while ai aggregated symbols use a "#" symbol (trying to be visually clear while also civ4-compatible). So for example maxwarrand 400 gandhi has a score of 0 normalized among all leaders (it is highest score i.e Gandhi is the least war-like leader(s (if others have a value of 400 too))) so his scale is "++++++++++" (100th percentile), while moctezuma has a warrand of 50, score 100 among all leaders (very war-like (in a "T.W war Likely" way highest leader(s (if other have same value of 50)))), so his scale is "" (0th percentile). Aggregated ai attributes follow a similar logic, so for example for the contact offer probabilites ai aggregated attribute, shaka has a "Tr Tech (30/5)" score of 79, meanings he is likely, relatively to all leaders, to contact you for a tech trade offer, and 79th percentile is a bit short of 80 but still 7 scales/symbols so he will be "#######". If the value is 50 sharp, for raw ai attributes or ai aggregated attributes, the symbol will also be "+++++" or "#####". This is especially important because the last symbol is "=====", which only happens if, for both raw ai attributes and ai aggregated attributes, if all leaders have strictly the same raw value for this attribute aggregated or raw is same in this case functionally, i.e. they'll both get an equal scale symbol, and precisely 5 scales, also all leaders will scale 50. So 50 "+++++", 50 "#####", and 50 "=====" all have different meanings, one being respectively only for raw ai attributes scoring of said leader, the other of similarly same ai aggregated value of said leader, while the last being the display of an equal value among all leaders for an easier read.

Sevopedia numTxt Legend (CONCEPT_SAS_SEVOPEDIA_NUMTXT_LEGEND)
######################################################################
== Sevopedia numTxt Legend ==

This page explains abbreviated labels shown in Sevopedia Building, Unit, Terrain, Feature, Bonus and Build pages.

== Unit/Building Civilization panels ==

- AllR: All Remaining Civilizations. The displayed generic Unit or Building is available to every civilization except those with a civilization-specific replacement or no mapping for that class. For example, the generic Swordsman is available to AllR, while Rome can train only its Legionary replacement instead.

== Building page numTxt ==

- InC: required in city (the prerequisite building must be in the same city).

- R: world-size requirement modifier (not random). This is the same R used inside AC N+R.

- AC N+R: required in all cities. N = base XML requirement, R = world-size requirement modifier from World Size iBuildingClassPrereqModifier (rounded up).

- Example for AC with N=2: Duel/Tiny (modifier 0) => 2; Standard (+38) => 3; Huge (+75) => 4.

- ACs: applies to all cities.

- AUC: applies to all eligible unit classes/combats.

- (!)Cvc: this prerequisite can be bypassed or altered by civic rules.

- OR: alternative prerequisite grouping; one of the OR-linked icons can satisfy that part.

- Plain number (for example 1, 2, 3): exact count linked to that icon (for example free specialists count).

- FBBR (when shown alone for free bonus count): world-size dependent free bonus count (iNumFreeBuildingBonuses). In this mod, for example: Standard=4, Huge=6.

== Unit/Terrain/Feature page numTxt ==

- +A/+D: attack and defense percent modifiers.

- +A/_: attack-only modifier.

- _/+D: defense-only modifier.

- _/_: no attack/defense modifier.

- +X%: single percent modifier (for example combat-class modifier).

- N: unit has free Navigator promotion.

- HM1, HM2, HM3: unit has free Hills Master tier 1/2/3. Contiguous tiers use ranges (for example HM1-3); gaps keep addition (for example HM1+3).

- W1, W2, W3: unit has free Woodsman tier 1/2/3. Combined forms (for example W1+2+3) indicate multiple free tiers.

== Bonus page numTxt ==

- Cons: the corporation consumes this bonus: a city needs it, or another of the corporation's inputs, for the corporation's output there.

- Prod: the corporation produces this bonus in the cities where it is present.

== Build page numTxt ==

- RF: Removes Feature. The action removes the feature shown in Result. For example, Drain Marsh removes Marsh.

== Cannot move / Cannot Enter (*) ==

- Land units can still enter such a tile on a route, for example a road through a Forest; naval units can enter it inside their own cultural borders.

- AdvCiv let every unit enter inside its own borders; AdvCiv-SAS restored the BtS rule for land units, since a Chariot does not cross a Forest more easily at home. See KI#1076 and the Main Changes Guide.

Sevopedia World Sizes Legend (CONCEPT_SAS_SEVOPEDIA_WORLD_SIZE_CHART_LEGEND)
######################################################################
== Sevopedia World Sizes Legend ==

The "Usual Grid Cells W x H" row shows each world size's default CIV4WorldInfo terrain-cell grid. It is indicative only - the final playable-plot dimensions depend on the map script.

- "WorldInfo Grid Cells*" multiplies those two WorldInfo values, and "Cells Per Default Player*" divides that product by the default player count. Neither row is a final playable-plot count.

- Without a custom size override, AdvCiv doubles each WorldInfo axis, producing four playable plots per WorldInfo cell. A map script may instead define getGridSize; AdvCiv multiplies each returned axis by four, and the script can choose different terrain-cell dimensions for its layout (e.g. Longworld stretches into a long, thin world).

- So effective sizes vary by script. At Large, for example, Pangaea (mostly land) is ~74x52, while PerfectMongoose is ~80x56: a more watery map needs a larger final grid to end up with about as many land plots as a land-heavy map like Pangaea gets from a smaller grid. Otherwise there would be too little land for the number of players this world size expects, leaving starts cramped and cities crowded. Conversely, an inhabitable land-heavy map should use a smaller grid.

- For your current game's actual playable-plot grid, open the Victory Screen (F8) then the Settings tab; the world-size line shows it, e.g. "Large World Size (80x56)".

Sevopedia Event Triggers Legend (CONCEPT_SAS_SEVOPEDIA_EVENT_TRIGGERS_LEGEND)
######################################################################
== Sevopedia Event Triggers Legend ==

This page explains the era groupings and abbreviations used in Sevopedia Event Triggers.

Era grouping:

- Any era (no tech requirement): triggers that can fire from turn 1 because they declare no tech prerequisite of any kind.

- Ancient / Classical / Medieval / ... : triggers whose earliest tech prerequisite is visible directly on the trigger XML, i.e. in its OrPreReqs or AndPreReqs tech list. These are "direct" era classifications - the gate is explicit on the trigger itself.

- Ancient (indirect) / Classical (indirect) / ... : triggers whose era was inferred via chain lookup because the trigger XML does not list a direct tech prereq. The era comes from one of: OtherPlayerHasTech (another player must have a tech), a prerequisite event trigger's inferred era, the required Civic's prereq tech, a required Building/Unit class's default building/unit prereq tech, a required Religion's prereq tech, or a required Corporation's prereq tech. These are "indirect" - the trigger never reads "requires Flight" directly, but it requires something that implies Flight.

Random event selection:

- iWeight and Active in Games affect the normal automatic random-event lottery. Manual or scripted triggering can still force an event trigger outside that lottery, so iWeight 0 or Active in Games 0% does not necessarily mean the XML entry is unused.

Requirements display:

- Requires (Buttons) shows linkable requirements such as techs, civics, buildings, units, religions, corporations, terrains, features, improvements, bonuses, and prerequisite event triggers.

- Requires (Text) shows numeric, flag, route, target-selection, and Python-gate constraints that have no clear pedia button target.

Numbered labels (numTxt) under buttons:

- Oth.P. : other player has this tech. Pairs with the OtherPlayerHasTech field. This tech does not need to be researched by the trigger's player - someone else in the game must have it. The label appears because the icon art is identical to a self-prereq tech icon and would otherwise be ambiguous.

- OR : appears between adjacent buttons in a Requires OR-prereq group of two or more techs, between same-field plot alternatives such as terrain/feature/improvement/bonus options, or between adjacent Obsolete with techs when multiple techs can obsolete the trigger. Means any single one satisfies that group; for Obsolete with, any single one blocks the trigger.

- No connector between adjacent buttons means AND: all shown requirements apply. AND is left implicit because writing "and" between every required icon makes dense panels harder to scan.
